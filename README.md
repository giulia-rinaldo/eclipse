# Minutes of totality


## The idea

*For a few minutes, the day goes dark.*

This interactive data story explores solar eclipses. It began with the total solar eclipse of 12 August 2026, which struck me with the remarkable power it had to move people. I was fascinated by how a celestial event, lasting only a few minutes, could bring so many people together and leave such a lasting impression. It made me wonder: just how rare is a moment like this?

I decided to follow that curiosity. The story starts by unpacking what a solar eclipse actually is, then maps where the next total eclipses will occur. From there, it looks back at what people have made of these extraordinary moments throughout history, exploring three events in particular: 585 BCE, 1919 and 2017. Finally, it looks ahead, tracing the 51 total solar eclipses still to come before 2100.

Created as part of *Introduction to Information Design* at Aalto University, October 2026, under the guidance of Professor Jonatan Hildén.


## Process

1. **Inspiration.** It all started with a simple question: how often do solar eclipses happen, and when will the next one occur?
2. **Research.** Gathering and exploring both quantitative data and qualitative sources to understand the scientific, historical and cultural dimensions of solar eclipses.
3. **Data and data management.** Interpreting and structuring the information, transforming NASA's eclipse catalogue into clear, manageable tables.
4. **UX/UI.** Designing the experience as a scrolling data story, guiding the reader through the narrative one chapter at a time. I started with paper sketches before moving into the digital layout and interactions.
5. **Aesthetics.** A black-and-white visual language inspired by the two protagonists: the Sun and the Moon.
6. **Development.** HTML, CSS and JavaScript.
