1 · Anatomy

Four ways the Moon crosses the Sun

A solar eclipse can happen only at New Moon, when the Moon passes between Earth and the Sun. It must also pass near one of two places where its orbit crosses Earth's. The Moon's shadow then sweeps across a narrow part of our planet. The kind of eclipse depends on which part of that shadow reaches you.

Total
The darkest part of the shadow, the umbra, reaches Earth. The Moon covers the Sun.
Annular
The Moon appears too small to cover the Sun. A bright ring remains around it.
Hybrid
The shadow shifts along its path: the eclipse is annular in some places and total in others.
Partial
The lighter outer shadow, the penumbra, reaches Earth. Part of the Sun stays visible.

Across five millennia, the four types appear in different numbers

Eclipses from 2001 to 2100, by magnitude

For total, annular and hybrid eclipses, magnitude compares the apparent sizes of the Moon and Sun. Above 1, the Moon appears larger; below 1, the Sun does. A hybrid shifts between the two along its path. For a partial eclipse, magnitude is the fraction of the Sun's diameter covered.
